About ResQNet

What this platform does, and — just as importantly — what it does not.

The problem

After a disaster, the bottleneck in relief work is rarely collecting reports. It is triage: deciding which of several thousand reports describes a family trapped under a collapsed roof, and which describes a damaged boundary wall that can safely wait a week. Done by hand, that decision is slow, inconsistent, and almost impossible to justify afterwards.

What ResQNet does

ResQNet manages the complete relief lifecycle — from a citizen's first report through to a completed payout — and connects the three groups involved: citizens, field officers, and government administrators.

  • Scores urgency against published rules. Each damage indicator carries a fixed weight, the weights are visible, and every score can be broken down line by line.
  • Dispatches the right officer. Availability, zone, distance and current workload — with the reasoning stored on the assignment.
  • Bases relief on verification, not claims. Compensation is calculated from what an officer confirmed on site.
  • Records everything. Every significant action is appended to a permanent history.
  • Works without a network. Reports and inspections are stored on the device and sent when connectivity returns.

Why the rules are visible

ResQNet deliberately uses a rule-based engine rather than machine learning. When a citizen asks why their case ranked below their neighbour's, a public relief programme has to be able to answer. "The model decided" is not an acceptable answer; a sum of published rules is.

What ResQNet is not

This is an academic project.
  • It is not connected to emergency dispatch services. Filing a report here does not summon help. In a life-threatening emergency, call your national emergency number.
  • Payout processing is simulated. No banking or treasury system is connected, and no money moves.
  • It is not an official government system and carries no authority to award relief.

Architecture

A Django monolith with Django REST Framework, PostgreSQL, and a server-rendered frontend using IndexedDB for offline storage and Leaflet.js for maps. There are no microservices, no message queues, and no external AI services — every piece of infrastructure present is there because a requirement demanded it.
