Privacy notice

What ResQNet stores about you, why, and who can see it.

This is an academic project. This notice describes how the software behaves; it is not a legal document and does not create obligations on any authority.

What is collected

  • Account details — name, username, email, phone number, address and district.
  • Damage reports — description, disaster and damage type, damage indicators, coordinates and address.
  • Photographs — images you upload, plus any GPS metadata already embedded in them by your camera.
  • Activity history — the actions taken on your case, who took them, and when.

Why it is collected

Location and damage details determine how urgently your case is handled and which officer is sent. Photographs are evidence for verification. Contact details allow an officer to reach you. The activity history exists so that relief decisions can be audited — including by you.

Who can see it

  • You — your own reports, inspections, compensation and case history.
  • The assigned field officer — the cases assigned to them, and nothing else. Officers cannot browse other officers' cases.
  • Administrators — all cases, because reviewing and approving relief is their function.

These boundaries are enforced by the server on every request. A citizen requesting another citizen's report receives nothing, regardless of what the interface shows.

Public statistics

The home page shows aggregate counts only — totals, resolution rate, officers deployed. It contains no names, no coordinates, and no case references.

Data stored on your device

When you fill in a report or inspection without a connection, it is stored in your browser's local database (IndexedDB) until it can be sent. It is removed from your device once the server confirms receipt. Clearing your browser data will delete anything not yet sent.

Retention

Cases and their audit history are retained for the life of the deployment. Audit records are append-only: they are never edited or deleted through the application.
